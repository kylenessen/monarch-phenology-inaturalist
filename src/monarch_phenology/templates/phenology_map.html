Reading this map

Each hexagon counts unique observations, not individual caterpillars, pupae, photos, or butterflies. Repeat visits to the same animal can be separate observations. The color bins stay fixed across weeks, years, stage filters, and zoom levels. Broader H3 resolutions use higher color breaks so large cells do not all saturate. Legend values are the lower bound of each color bin. A darker hexagon means more records, not an estimate of population density.

Individual years use consecutive seven-day windows starting January 1, with a short final window. The pooled seasonal cycle aligns month and day on a leap-year calendar. The February 26 to March 3 bin has six days in non-leap years. Pooled counts are summed across years, not annual averages. Recent years have more observation effort, and the current year is incomplete.

Only unambiguous, undisputed iNaturalist Life Stage annotations are included. Eggs and adults are excluded. These are community annotations, not Jev predictions, and have not received a separate expert audit. A pupa label alone does not distinguish an occupied chrysalis from an empty one.

Obscured or private locations are excluded. The default excludes reported accuracy worse than 1 km but includes unstated accuracy. Choose “≤1 km only” to exclude those unstated locations too. Fine hexagons show the reported location, not certainty about its exact position. High resolutions can be much finer than the location accuracy. Choosing a smaller grid does not improve the underlying coordinates. All 16 H3 resolutions are available. Areas shown are global averages and vary geographically.

Sources iNaturalist observations and H3 cell sizes. Map tiles by OpenStreetMap contributors. Built locally without model calls.
