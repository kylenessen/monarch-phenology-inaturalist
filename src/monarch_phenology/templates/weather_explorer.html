Developed areas or natural cover?

Developed includes all four NLCD developed classes, including open space such as lawns and parks. Cropland and pasture remain separate. Natural cover describes the mapped surface, not whether a site is untouched or suitable monarch habitat.

Definitions, sources, and interpretation

The same fixed 600-observation stratified sample is used throughout. It represents 81,214 eligible human-labeled larva and pupa records from 2001 through 2025 with public coordinates and known uncertainty no greater than 1 km. Sampling weights correct the deliberate oversampling of sparse region, season, and stage groups. They do not correct observation effort, missing weather, or geographic land-cover coverage. These are observations, not counts of animals.

A 14-day window means days D−14 through D−1. Median minimum is the median of those 14 daily minimum temperatures. Median maximum is the median of their daily maxima. For 14 values, the middle two are averaged. Seven-day windows use D−7 through D−1. Each metric requires a complete window for its constituent variables. Daymet omits December 31 in leap years. Missing days are not interpolated. If an expanded request fails, previously cached pilot temperatures are retained and identified, while the other variables remain unavailable. Medians reduce the influence of a single extreme day. They are not a correction for errors or confounding.

Daymet V4 R1 supplies estimates at 1 km, not direct measurements at each observation. Vapor pressure is actual atmospheric water-vapor pressure, not relative humidity. Shortwave radiation is a daylight average. Daily solar energy is radiation times day length, divided by one million. Radiation, vapor pressure, and snowpack depend partly on the temperature and precipitation model inputs. AET has been removed from this explorer.

USGS Annual NLCD Collection 1.2 provides 30 m land cover for the contiguous U.S. We sample the category at the reported coordinate in the observation year through the USGS MRLC service. No prior-year substitution is used. Canada, Mexico, and other uncovered areas remain unavailable. A 30 m pixel can be much smaller than the coordinate uncertainty. Use the 30 m uncertainty filter as a sensitivity check. This is a point category, not the land-cover composition of the surrounding area, and does not identify milkweed or nectar plants. Official category definitions.

The simple habitat comparison groups NLCD classes 21, 22, 23, and 24 as Developed. Pasture/hay (81) and cultivated crops (82) are Agriculture. Other recognized classes are Natural cover, including forest, shrub, grassland, wetlands, water, snow, and barren land. Unavailable categories are kept separate. The default comparison includes only Developed and Natural cover. Original categories remain available in filters, inspection, and downloads. This is a land-cover grouping, not evidence of habitat selection.

Histogram percentages and cumulative distributions normalize each displayed group separately. Box summaries use the selected weighting for their 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles, with whiskers at the 5th and 95th percentiles. Seasonal lines show medians among observations in each month, pooled across selected years. Months with fewer than three valid observations are omitted. Scatterplot correlations are descriptive Pearson correlations, using the selected weights. No significance tests or causal claims are implied. Daily temperature plots always show the complete fetched 14-day period.

The existing extreme-temperature audit checked only 26 observations against ERA5-Land. One showed a large disagreement. The audit flag applies to its original 14-day extrema, not a new audit of every variable. Unannotated does not mean alive. Pupa annotations may include dead or empty chrysalises. Presence-only observations cannot establish absence, breeding limits, or survival thresholds.

Summary CSV temperature values are Celsius. Daily CSV units are tmin/tmax in °C, prcp in mm/day, vp in Pa, srad in W/m², swe in kg/m², and dayl in seconds. Filtered CSV values follow the displayed temperature units and selected window. Original service responses, query parameters, and metadata remain cached locally.
